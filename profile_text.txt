WHO WE AREDsectors was founded on a simple observation: development partners in Bangladesh increasingly require research and advisory partners who combine methodological rigour with genuine field access to marginalised and hard-to-reach populations, and who can meet the compliance standards of institutional donors without sacrificing speed or contextual insight.Our Board of Directors brings together, in a single firm, capabilities that are ordinarily distributed across several: a USAID Project Design Team Lead who facilitated a portfolio of approximately 240 projects and activities valued at close to USD 3 billion; a World Bank Social Development Specialist who delivered gender and GBV technical leadership across 50+ sovereign operations in Bangladesh and Bhutan; a UN Women and Oxfam gender and humanitarian specialist with 27 years of leadership in gender-responsive DRR and climate resilience; a communications and knowledge-management specialist who has led or contributed to over 30 research studies for UNAIDS, ActionAid, Save the Children, Bandhu and Justice and Care; an academic researcher in gender, media and discourse analysis; and a certified enterprise architect with over 35 end-to-end ERP implementations for clients including Save the Children, CIRDAP and the Government of Bangladesh.The result is a firm that can design a study, secure ethical clearance, reach key populations, analyse and write to donor standard, communicate the findings at scale, and build the digital systems that carry the results into practice — under one contract.What Distinguishes UsDonor-compliance fluency. Directors have sat on the donor side of the table — designing Project Appraisal Documents (PADs), serving as AOR/COR, and assessing gender tags — and understand exactly what World Bank, ADB, USAID, FCDO, DFAT, ECHO and UN procurement requires of a deliverable.Access to hard-to-reach populations. A decade of HIV and key-population programming has given the firm working relationships with community-led organisations serving people who inject drugs, female sex workers, transgender/hijra and SOGIESC communities — populations that conventional survey firms cannot reliably reach.Gender and safeguards as core competence, not an add-on. Three of our directors are recognised specialists in gender mainstreaming, GBV/SEA/SH risk management, and IFC Performance Standards / World Bank ESS compliance.Research-to-communication continuity. We do not stop at the report. Our campaigns have reached upwards of 60 million people nationwide, and our BCC/IEC materials are in use in national programmes under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.Digital delivery capability. In-house ERP, platform and AI-integration expertise allows us to deliver digital outreach models, MIS systems and e-commerce platforms rather than merely recommending them.3. OUR APPROACHPhaseWhat We Do1. Inception and DesignClarify the evaluation or research question against the client's decision needs; agree the theory of change and evaluation matrix; design sampling and instruments; secure ethical clearance and informed-consent protocols; produce an inception report to donor standard.2. Access and Data CollectionDeploy trained, gender-balanced enumeration teams; work through community-led organisations to reach key and hard-to-reach populations; apply safeguarding, PSEA and do-no-harm protocols throughout; supervise fieldwork with real-time quality assurance.3. Analysis and ValidationTriangulate quantitative, qualitative and digital/netnographic data; test findings against alternative explanations; hold validation workshops with the client, government counterparts and the communities concerned.4. Reporting and UptakeDeliver donor-standard reports, policy briefs and dissemination products; convert findings into BCC/IEC materials, campaigns and training modules where required; support the client to take the evidence into policy dialogue.5. InstitutionalisationBuild the client's own capacity — training, toolkits, MIS and digital systems — so that the evidence base can be maintained after the assignment closes.11.
